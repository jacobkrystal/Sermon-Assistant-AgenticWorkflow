## 1. Research Background and Motivation

### 1.1 The Problem

The Philippines faces a growing chronic kidney disease (CKD) crisis. The Philippine Renal Disease Registry reports a steady rise in end-stage renal disease (ESRD) patients requiring regular dialysis, yet the quality and accessibility of dialysis services remain unevenly distributed. Most Philippine dialysis centers operate as clinical facilities with minimal attention to patient experience, service design, or complementary care integration. This creates a gap between the medical necessity of dialysis and the holistic well-being of chronic patients who spend 3-4 hours, three times a week, in these facilities.

### 1.2 The Entrepreneurial Context

The researcher is a Korean immigrant entrepreneur in the Philippines with 16+ years of multi-industry experience spanning hospitality (hotel management), food & beverage (coffee franchise — both master franchise and micro-cafe models), traditional medicine (acupuncture), digital health (AI-powered patient self-diagnosis and telemedicine application), and education (international kindergarten). This diverse portfolio converges around a central venture: **establishing a Philippine dialysis center modeled on Korean facility and service standards, with hospitality-grade service design integrated from the construction phase**.

This is not a case of unrelated diversification. Each business component plays a strategic role in an integrated healthcare service ecosystem:

| Component | Ecosystem Role |
|-----------|---------------|
| Hotel experience | Service mindset DNA — embedded into all healthcare touchpoints |
| Korean dialysis benchmarks | Facility design and clinical service standards |
| Coffee franchise (₱1M in-hospital model) | Patient comfort, waiting area experience, revenue diversification |
| Acupuncture clinic | Complementary care for chronic pain and dialysis side-effects |
| AI health app | Patient empowerment through self-triage and telemedicine |
| International kindergarten | Community anchor, family trust, health education channel |

### 1.3 Research Motivation

The researcher's unique position — as both the ecosystem builder and the study's primary informant — offers rare access to longitudinal, first-person data on cross-industry knowledge transfer, service design decisions, and business model innovation in an emerging market healthcare context. This insider perspective, combined with rigorous methodological safeguards against bias, can produce practitioner-relevant knowledge that purely external research cannot.

## 2. Research Purpose and Questions

### 2.1 Purpose Statement

The purpose of this study is to explore how a multi-industry entrepreneurial background (hospitality, F&B, traditional medicine, digital health, education) contributes to building an integrated healthcare service ecosystem centered on dialysis care in the Philippines, and to examine the resulting impact on patient experience and business sustainability.

### 2.2 Research Questions

**RQ1 (Knowledge Transfer):**
How does cross-industry entrepreneurial experience (hotel, F&B, traditional medicine, digital health) transfer into the design and operation of a dialysis-centered healthcare service ecosystem in an emerging market?

**RQ2 (Patient Experience):**
How do complementary ecosystem services (in-hospital coffee shop, acupuncture, AI self-diagnosis app) influence the patient experience and perceived care quality for chronic dialysis patients?

**RQ3 (Business Sustainability):**
To what extent does the integrated service ecosystem model demonstrate financial sustainability advantages compared to standalone dialysis operations?

## 3. Preliminary Literature Review

### 3.1 Key Theoretical Domains

**3.1.1 Business Ecosystem Theory (Moore, 1993; Iansiti & Levien, 2004)**
Moore's concept of a business ecosystem — where firms co-evolve capabilities around a central innovation — provides the macro-level framework. The dialysis center acts as the "keystone" around which complementary services (coffee, acupuncture, digital health) orbit, creating value that no single component could generate alone.

**3.1.2 Cross-Industry Knowledge Transfer (Argote & Ingram, 2000; Enkel & Gassmann, 2010)**
The transfer of service design knowledge from hospitality to healthcare is a specific case of cross-industry innovation. Enkel & Gassmann's work on "cross-industry analogies" provides a framework for analyzing how hotel service standards are recontextualized for clinical settings.

**3.1.3 Servicescape Theory (Bitner, 1992; Rosenbaum & Massiah, 2011)**
Bitner's servicescape framework — how physical environments shape customer (patient) behavior and satisfaction — is directly relevant to the researcher's approach of embedding service design from the construction phase. This extends beyond typical healthcare facility design research by including the full ecosystem (café space, acupuncture area, digital touchpoints).

**3.1.4 Patient Experience in Chronic Care (Beryl Institute; Doyle et al., 2013)**
Dialysis patients represent a unique case: they are long-term, repeat users of healthcare facilities. Their experience is shaped not just by clinical outcomes but by the physical environment, wait times, complementary services, and emotional support — all areas where the ecosystem approach intervenes.

**3.1.5 Immigrant Entrepreneurship (Portes & Zhou, 1993; Kloosterman & Rath, 2001)**
The Korean-in-Philippines context adds a layer of immigrant entrepreneurship theory, particularly around how ethnic knowledge networks (Korean dialysis center benchmarks) and cross-cultural service standards create competitive advantages.

**3.1.6 Digital Health in Emerging Markets (WHO, 2019; Labrique et al., 2018)**
The AI self-diagnosis and telemedicine component connects to the growing literature on mobile health (mHealth) in low- and middle-income countries, specifically patient empowerment through self-triage tools.

### 3.2 Research Gap

While individual domains (healthcare servicescape, immigrant entrepreneurship, digital health in developing countries) have been studied, **no existing research examines how a single entrepreneur integrates knowledge from hospitality, F&B, traditional medicine, digital health, and education into a cohesive healthcare service ecosystem in an emerging market.** This gap is both theoretical (how cross-industry knowledge combines at the ecosystem level) and practical (how to build such a model).

## 4. Proposed Methodology

### 4.1 Research Design

**Embedded Single-Case Study with Mixed Methods** (Yin, 2018; Creswell & Plano Clark, 2018)

Rationale: The researcher's ecosystem represents a unique, revelatory case. The embedded design allows analyzing both the whole ecosystem (RQ1) and individual components' effects (RQ2, RQ3).

### 4.2 Data Collection

| Data Source | Method | RQ Served | Sample/Scope |
|-------------|--------|-----------|-------------|
| Researcher's experience | Autoethnographic reflection + structured journaling | RQ1 | 16 years, documented retrospectively |
| Business documents | Document analysis (construction plans, service protocols, financial records) | RQ1, RQ3 | All available records |
| Dialysis patients | Survey (SERVQUAL adapted) + semi-structured interviews | RQ2 | n=150-200 (survey), 15-20 (interviews) |
| Staff members | Semi-structured interviews | RQ1, RQ2 | 10-15 across ecosystem businesses |
| Comparative financial data | Secondary data analysis | RQ3 | Ecosystem center vs. 3-5 standalone centers |
| AI app usage data | Platform analytics | RQ2 | App user logs (anonymized) |

### 4.3 Data Analysis

- **Qualitative**: Thematic analysis (Braun & Clarke, 2006) for interviews and autoethnographic data; process tracing for knowledge transfer pathways
- **Quantitative**: Descriptive statistics + regression analysis for survey data; financial ratio comparison for RQ3
- **Integration**: Triangulation protocol — converging qualitative and quantitative findings through a joint display matrix

### 4.4 Validity and Reliability

| Threat | Mitigation |
|--------|-----------|
| Insider bias (researcher = entrepreneur) | External auditor review; member checking with staff and patients; reflexivity journal; triangulation with quantitative data |
| Single-case generalizability | Analytical generalization to theory (not statistical); detailed thick description for transferability |
| Recall bias (retrospective data) | Corroboration with contemporaneous documents (contracts, photos, financial records) |
| Social desirability (patient surveys) | Anonymous surveys; neutral interviewers (not the researcher) |

### 4.5 Ethical Considerations

- IRB approval required for patient surveys and interviews
- Informed consent for all participants
- Anonymization of patient data and financial specifics
- Researcher positionality statement in methodology chapter

## 5. Expected Contributions

### 5.1 Theoretical Contributions

1. **Extends Business Ecosystem Theory** to healthcare entrepreneurship in emerging markets, demonstrating how non-healthcare industries (hospitality, F&B, education) create value in a healthcare-centered ecosystem
2. **Bridges Cross-Industry Knowledge Transfer and Servicescape Theory** by showing how hospitality service design knowledge is physically embedded into healthcare facility construction — a process-level contribution
3. **Adds to Immigrant Entrepreneurship literature** by documenting how ethnic knowledge networks (Korean dialysis standards) combine with adopted-country market knowledge to create hybrid service models

### 5.2 Practical Contributions

1. **Replicable Framework**: A step-by-step model for healthcare entrepreneurs seeking to build integrated service ecosystems in emerging markets
2. **Construction-Phase Service Design Guide**: Specific design principles for embedding service quality into healthcare facility construction (not retrofitting)
3. **₱1M In-Hospital Micro-Café Model**: A cost-effective complementary service model with patient experience data
4. **AI Self-Triage Integration Guide**: Practical insights for deploying patient-facing AI tools in dialysis care settings

### 5.3 Policy Implications

- Recommendations for Philippine health regulators on patient experience standards for dialysis facilities
- Evidence base for complementary care integration in chronic disease management

## 8. Key Terms

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Integrated Healthcare Service Ecosystem** | A network of complementary businesses (medical, F&B, traditional medicine, digital health, education) coordinated around a core healthcare service to enhance patient experience and business sustainability |
| **Cross-Industry Knowledge Transfer** | The process by which knowledge, practices, and service standards from one industry (e.g., hospitality) are adapted and applied in another (e.g., healthcare) |
| **Servicescape** | The physical environment in which a service is delivered, including facility design, ambient conditions, spatial layout, and signage (Bitner, 1992) |
| **Keystone Business** | The central firm in a business ecosystem around which other participants co-evolve (Iansiti & Levien, 2004) |
| **Patient Experience** | The sum of all interactions shaped by an organization's culture that influence patient perceptions across the continuum of care (Beryl Institute) |
